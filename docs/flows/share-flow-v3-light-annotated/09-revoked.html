    Two queries, and the first one deliberately does not filter on expires_at. `WHERE expires_at > now()` would fold an expired share into a missing one and cost the page the difference between 410 and 404 - which is the difference between the two dead-end screens. The row is fetched, then the expiry is compared.

    Open question. As drawn this is the response to a revoked share. Under the TTL decision there is no revoke: the same screen becomes *expired*, served as 410 when `expires_at` has passed. That is why the manifest query does not filter on expiry - a filtered query could only answer 404 here, and 404 is the other screen.
