Share flow, on Mercury light

Handing a batch of invoices to someone who has no account: an accountant, a co-founder, a client. The light twin of share-flow-v3: the same markup and the same copy on the light palette from mercury-ui-light. Everything that differed between the two folders lived in tokens-v3.css, which is the whole point of landing the tokens once — until revision 2 drew two decisions the dark twin predates: expiry replaced revoke and a share now says who made it, so there are fifteen screens here and fourteen there. Each frame is a real page linked against the app’s own tokens.css and dashboard.css, so these cannot drift from the product.

Two clicks to a live link

Update, then Share. The link is minted and on the clipboard before any dialog asks anything. Five things deliberately do not exist, because each one is a decision the user would otherwise make before getting their link:
  1. No share dialog. The click is the share. The popover reports, it does not ask.
  2. No expiry picker, password, or permission levels. Every link gets the same seven days and there is no switch at all: no revoke, and nothing to set.
  3. No separate preview. “Open the link” opens the exact URL the recipient gets.
  4. No picking. The link is the batch: no checkboxes, no partial zip. Wrong batch? Tick rows on the dashboard and share again; it is still two clicks.
  5. No attachments. The mail carries the summary and a button. 12 MB does not.

What v3 changed

The full diagnosis is in audit.md. In one list:
  • Light, and strictly flat. Every shadow token resolves to none, and the grain overlay, the ambient washes and the gradients went with them. Hierarchy is contrast, space and type.
  • One accent, and it is blue. #5266eb on the two real actions and the focus ring. Errors get the one status colour Section 1.B allows; nothing else is coloured at all.
  • Two columns instead of an overlap. A sticky rail carries the filename, the facts and the download; the manifest scrolls beside it.
  • Nothing loops. The pulsing dot and the skeleton shimmer are gone.
  • The fan is gone. Overlapped paper needs a shadow to read as a stack, so the documents became the tile the product renders when a thumbnail is missing.
  • No caps, no weights above 500, nothing under 12px, no em dashes.

The Share button

One new control in the toolbar the table already has. It shares the ticked rows, or everything shown when nothing is ticked, so reusing the checkbox column means the selection step costs no new UI at all. It is a labelled, filled button: as a bare glyph the one thing this screen exists for was its least visible control, and it sat beside a download arrow one stroke away from its own. That download icon is gone with it - the share page it opens is where the whole batch is downloaded, and the per-row arrow still covers a single invoice.

Redressed rather than rebuilt: same markup, new tokens. The ten vendor brand colours are gone, because ten saturated fills is ten accents and the system allows one. Icon buttons and the pager are pill, the table header lost its tint, and the uppercase labels came back to sentence case.

The row, opened

Owner open full screen ↗

A branch off step 1 rather than a step through it: sharing never passes through here. The row opens in place, so the rows around it stay readable and there is nothing to dismiss. Clicking it again closes it.

The panel answers one question, which is whether the parser read the document correctly, so it puts the document beside the fields taken off it. The amount leads, because it is the figure the row was opened to check, and it is not repeated in the list below. Fields the extractor came back empty on are not rendered: templates are per issuer, and a fixed list would report the template’s coverage rather than the invoice.

The open row is not tinted with the accent. A ticked row is, and disclosure was borrowing the same colour, so two unrelated states looked identical. The row and its panel take the page colour instead, cut into the card as a well, which also makes them read as one block. The document panel is the state where the page render has not arrived; the product drops the vendor’s own PDF into that box.

2

Link created

POST /shares Owner open full screen ↗

The link already exists and is already on the clipboard, so there is nothing to confirm. The response also carries an owner_key, which this browser keeps in localStorage and never shows anyone; it is what gates sending the mail and correcting the name on the link later. The snapshot of invoice ids is frozen here, so a later scan cannot silently widen a link that has already gone out — and so are the two fields that say who shared it, taken from the connected mailbox at this moment. The popover names the day the link stops working, because that is now the only thing that ever ends it.

Opening the link is the button and sending it by email is a text link. A matched pair of filled and outlined buttons reads as two equal decisions, and only one of them is the point. While the popover is open, the share control itself carries the one tinted state on the screen.

While the manifest loads

Anyone with the link new in v2 open full screen ↗

A manifest of 37 rows is a request, and a request has a duration. The skeleton is shaped like the page it stands in for, rail and tiles and rows, so nothing jumps when the data lands.

It no longer shimmers. v2 ran a gradient sweep across every placeholder for as long as the request took, which is a perpetual loop dressed as feedback; the shape alone already reads as not here yet, and aria-busy says it to everyone else.

4

The share page

GET /s/{token} Anyone with the link open full screen ↗

The page is two columns now. The left rail holds what the batch is and the one thing to do with it: the filename at display size, four labelled facts, the download, and for the owner the live state with the date it ends, the name recipients will see, and the send. It sticks, so the answer and the action stay on screen through 37 rows. v2 stacked all of that down one column and lifted the document card 44px into the band above it to break the stripes, which only worked because three layered shadows told you which card was in front.

The right column is what is inside the download: a pdf part and a csv part, each named and chipped with its file type, under a rail already chipped zip. No sentence says “you are downloading PDFs and a spreadsheet”; the layout says it.

The five thumbnails no longer fan. Overlapped, rotated paper reads as a stack because of the shadow between each sheet, and there are none here, so the documents became the tile the product actually renders when a page-1 thumbnail is missing: the vendor's initials on a plain panel. Six cells for six things, one of them the count of the other 32.

The table is a sheet, not a working surface: hairline grid, 34px rows, tabular mono, the zip’s own filenames, counts on the last line, and a sticky header so the column names survive row 30. Row 6 shows hyphens where Bolt’s ride receipt carries no invoice number and no VAT split, which is the real shape of the data. The ninth column is not data: it counts what the CSV has and the page does not (+15 in csv), and narrow screens drop columns into that count so the number stays true at every width.

Correcting the name

PATCH /s/{token} Owner open full screen ↗

A branch off step 4, and usually skipped. The name a recipient is greeted by comes from the connected mailbox, which is the only thing this product knows about its user: the mailbox’s own display name if it carries one, and otherwise the local part of the address — martin from martin@kirov.dev, and martinvkirov from a Gmail address, which is the case that makes the point. It is recognisable enough to send under and wrong often enough to want fixing, so it is shown, not hidden, with one control beside it.

The edit happens in the rail, in place, with the page it changes still on screen behind it. Only the name is editable: the address underneath it is the mailbox that will actually send, and letting anyone type that would make the line a claim rather than a fact. The correction is kept for the next link too, so this is a screen most people see once.

Preparing the download

The zip is streamed and never assembled on disk, so its size is known before the first byte, which means this can be a determinate bar rather than a spinner. The panel replaces the button in place, so nothing on the page moves, and it names what is being built: 36 documents and the CSV.

The bar is a flat fill of the accent on the canvas-deep track. v2 ran a gradient from one accent step to another inside it, which is decoration on a control whose whole job is to report a number.

6

Composer

Owner open full screen ↗

One field the user must fill: the address. Subject and body are already drafted, and the mail goes out from a connected mailbox, so Unipile already holds those credentials and there is no second OAuth and no Gmail client to write.

Labels sit above their fields now, which is the shape a form is supposed to have; v2 ran them down the left on a fixed 52px column, which is the settings-row pattern. From states the answer rather than asking a question: the address as text, the way it would read on a sent message, with a caret beside it if there is anywhere else to send from. It is a span, not a select, and its label is a span too, because a <label for> pointing at no control promises a screen reader something that is not there.

The draft is right there underneath, in an iframe over the real rendered mail: the same document the API sends, not a mock-up of it.

Choosing the mailbox

GET /accounts Owner open full screen ↗

The caret exists only because two mailboxes are connected. With one it is not rendered, because a control that leads nowhere is worse than no control. With two, personal and billing say, which one sends is a real question, and it decides where the reply lands.

The current mailbox stays in the list and is ticked, so the menu answers which am I on? as well as what else is there? The choice is remembered, so it is asked once rather than every time.

The address needs fixing

Owner new in v2 open full screen ↗

Checked on blur, before anything is sent: aria-invalid, aria-describedby, a tinted border, and a sentence that says what is wrong and guesses the fix rather than “invalid input”. Send is disabled while it stands, as aria-disabled rather than a dead button that fails silently on click.

This is one of exactly two places in the flow that carry a status colour. v2 drew errors in the accent, which read correctly only because the accent happened to be red; against an electric blue the same treatment says nothing at all, so Section 1.B’s one danger token earns its place here and on the failed send. The light twin takes that token an octave down: a light red on a light page is 2.4:1.

Sent

POST /s/{token}/email Owner open full screen ↗

Confirmation names who it went to and which mailbox it came from, because “sent” alone leaves the user checking their own Sent folder. Sending again to someone else is one click and reuses the same link.

No green, and no exclamation mark. Success has no colour in this system at all: the tick is ink on the neutral lift, and the sentence carries the rest.

The email

Recipient open full screen ↗

Shaped like a receipt: a band that recedes, one heading, a short label/value block, one action. No thumbnails, no vendor list, no marketing, because the page has all of that and an inbox is not where anyone reviews 37 invoices.

600px, tables, inlined styles, plain-text alternative: the format that survives Outlook, and the one carve-out where literal hex is correct. The band is no longer a slab of the accent: the accent is rationed to actions, and this mail has exactly one. No attachment, either. 12 MB bounces, lands in spam and goes stale; a link does none of that.

Recipient’s view

The same page as screen 4, minus the owner block at the foot of the rail. No login wall, no “request access”, nothing to install. Because owner and recipient share one page, there is no what-they-will-see mode to keep in step, and no doubt in the owner’s mind about whether the preview was accurate.

v2 put the owner’s controls in a sticky bar across the top of the window, which was a second sticky element and an offset the sheet’s own sticky header had to read off the page. In the rail they cost neither.

13

Expired

GET /s/{token} → 410 Anyone with the link open full screen ↗

Says so plainly rather than 404ing. The recipient knows the link once worked, so pretending it never existed reads as a bug, and naming the day it lapsed tells them what to ask for. A dead end needs a way out, so it has two: mail the sender, or go back. The mark beside the heading is a clock and it is neutral, because nobody turned this link off — the seven days it was given ran out, which is a state and not an error.

A 22-character token in an email is a token that gets broken across two lines, so the likeliest 404 in this product has a known cause, and the page names it instead of saying “page not found”.
